# Final second selection: independent image-only review

## Scope and verdict

Inspected all eight actual JPG screenshots in `evidence/final2-images/` using `view_image` with `detail=original`: `scene0..3-group1/2.jpg`. Only those images were used. No source, metrics, author ratings, JSON, or other reviews were read; no product files were edited. A–E below refer to this package.

**No visible material blockers found in the submitted four scenes. All five preserve the required visible information roles, and the selection contains five sufficiently distinct visual proposals.** This supports a bounded visual freeze of the submitted images. It does not establish technical acceptance, physical Garmin readability, geographic accuracy, or user preference.

## Role completeness

Across A–E in each scene I can see:

- Complete time digits, including `18:38`, `04:44`, `00:01`, and `12:34`.
- Four separate configurable numeric fields with identifying pictograms; no field visibly disappears or merges with another.
- Rocket graphic, rocket name, company, and mission purpose.
- Country and launch-place label.
- Countdown in scenes 0–2; explicit `Время уточняется` in scene3 instead of an invented numeric countdown.
- Geography with a visible marker, separated from the text.

Scene1 shows the long RU company `Лаборатория небесной механики` and purpose `Калибровка звёздных датчиков`. A/D/E wrap these into multiple lines while B/C use wider single lines. The visible endings are preserved; the location `Индонезия · Тест Парус` does not cover the purpose.

Scene2 preserves `Cedar Mk II`, `Distant Orbit Research Centre`, `Auroral spectrum survey`, `Norway · Test Cedar`, and the long EN countdown. E wraps the company and retains `Centre`. All five keep the four relatively wide values visible.

Scene3 preserves `Вектор 27 Heavy`, `Орбитальные системы Меридиан`, `Проверка межспутниковой связи`, `Турция · Тест Лира`, and the unknown-time phrase. A/D/E use multiline details without an observed overlap. The unknown-time phrase is less prominent than the numeric countdown but remains visible.

These observations concern the strings actually shown. Without reading the underlying data, completeness relative to an external source or arbitrary future text cannot be certified.

| **E** | Full-face Earth as the scene, time on its own panel, central mission passport, then values, location, and countdown. These are five different arrangements of priorities, grouping, and geographic emphasis, not five palette substitutions. A/D both start with time but use different value grouping and geography. B/C both begin with mission identity but place purpose, time, values, and map differently. E makes the Earth the overall scene. This is enough distinct utility for a five-proposal visual shortlist.

My editorial preference remains for the calmer A/B, with C as a more expressive central-clock alternative. D/E are useful additional directions and should not be removed merely for being busier. No measured ranking of speed, accuracy, or appeal follows from these images.

## Geography and marker separation

The marker is visible in every screenshot. A/B place it inside a separate globe; C places it in the map to the right of the central clock; D places it on a lower geographic area left of the mission/location text; E places it right of the mission panel.

No marker is visibly hidden by clock digits, black mission panels, location labels, or countdown text. In D scene1/3 it aligns near the last purpose line, but horizontal separation is retained. In E a narrow geographic strip remains between the mission panel and marker, sufficient to see the marker distinctly in this package.

Seeing a marker does not prove its coordinates, the projection, or the correspondence between the graphic and named country/place. Those properties were not investigated.

## Blockers and recommendations

**Observed blockers: none.** No missing time digit, visibly clipped shown text, hidden required field, text outside the circle, or marker/text collision was found. No mandatory cosmetic change is justified before freezing these submitted images.

Keep the structural differences instead of forcing a common layout. If future text or fonts change, the first areas to inspect are the bottom of A/D's multiline purpose and the boundary between E's mission panel and value row. These are risk boundaries, not current failures.

The status signs have different shapes and change presence between scenarios: two signs, one sign, or none. Thus the shown changes are not conveyed solely by color. The full state vocabulary and meaning of each sign are not established by this package. D's signs sit between value columns; the other proposals generally place them at the top, so product-level semantics should remain clear despite that placement difference.

Some rocket silhouettes contain a visible `?`. It can suggest an unknown model, but that interpretation is not guaranteed without explanation or user observation. The rocket role itself remains visible.

## Unmeasured limits

- Physical Garmin rendering, distance, motion, sunlight, font rasterization, and actual on-wrist readability.
- Child/adult preference, comprehension of signs and rocket details, reading speed, and error rates.
- Geographic and source-data correctness.
- Other languages, longer strings, wider numbers, all configurations, and all status combinations.
- AOD quality: the small dark previews below the main circles are insufficient for acceptance.
- Animation, performance, battery, refresh behavior, and hardware compatibility.

**Final image-only conclusion:** A/B/C/D/E have complete visible roles and meaningful structural variety in all four supplied scenes, including unknown time. There is no observed visual blocker to retaining this five-proposal selection. Technical acceptance remains a separate evidence question.
