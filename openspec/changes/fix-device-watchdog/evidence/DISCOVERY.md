## Historical investigation checkpoint

Intermediate staging alone is insufficient: whole recursive catalog/feed parse remains indivisible. Native VM stack depth is also a constraint: adding helper wrappers to existing synchronous parse/admission caused an observed stack overflow on the captured feed (`prepared4.log`, isolated Linux simulator). Resumable tokenization must preserve nested ordering, per-node owners, spans, exact integer lexemes, escaped strings and existing byte/depth/node contracts, as well as lifecycle interruption and recovery. A single call to the existing parser from a timer is not an execution budget. As of this checkpoint the intermediate patch is unfinished and unsafe to deliver.

Known WIP defects beyond parser equivalence: the temporary admission fallback now attempts date, time, countdown, selected launch text and coordinates; it does not provide the required fallback map and its layout and runtime remain unverified, so it cannot be delivered in this form. Prepared packet promotion duplicates the synchronous body to avoid added VM stack frames and needs independent equivalence tests. Its parser and hash continuation have not been compiled. The fresh-input failure/marker-replacement and mode/hide/settings transitions need actual runtime tests. The original synchronous cache startup, strict restore, background and render owners are unchanged and remain within the requested scope.

## Current status

The WIP statements above describe the early checkpoint, not the final source. Foreground admission, strict restore and descriptor preparation now use resumable jobs, the unreachable synchronous foreground chain was removed, and current validation and simulator results are recorded in `PROGRESS.md`, `CALLERS.md` and `verification.json`. Subsequent stock simulator runs did reveal OOM under warm overlap; lazy map metadata and resource suspension addressed the finite captured-payload case. The fallback while a job runs is deliberately minimal, and returns to the normal renderer after completion. It is not a claim that an unknown source precision yields seconds or that physical hardware has passed.
