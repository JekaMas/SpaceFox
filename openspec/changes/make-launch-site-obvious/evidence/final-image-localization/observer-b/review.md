# Independent image-only review

All 60 trials were viewed individually in the supplied order at their original 390, 222, or 195 pixel dimensions. No zoom, upscaling, montage, source, truth, parent-folder metadata, or other review was used. Answers were locked in `answers.json` before this review was written.

The recorded observations contain 30 located launch-site markers, 26 faces with no visible geographic marker, and 4 cases where I could not reliably locate a point. These are observer judgments, not truth-based accuracy results.

## Visible cues

- Yellow centers inside white target rings were easy to distinguish from the dark globe or map. Their position could be reported even in the smallest supplied faces.
- Yellow or orange teardrop pins clearly indicated map locations. Reported coordinates use the center of the pin head, not its bottom tip.
- Globe-only `СТАРТ` labels suggested a launch context but did not identify a distinct point. I recorded these as unable rather than deriving coordinates from text or geography.
- Crossed-location symbols conveyed an unavailable or absent geographic point. The smallest boxed symbol was initially ambiguous enough to warrant an unable judgment in one trial.
- Dark maps without a ring or pin did not provide a reliable launch point. Place names and country labels were never used to manufacture a coordinate.

## Qualitative limits

Target rings communicate location through contrast and shape, but their launch-specific meaning is contextual when an explicit LAUNCH or СТАРТ label is absent. A map point alone could represent another geographic event in a different application.

The text-only START globes are the main localization ambiguity: the text supplies a concept while the globe supplies a region, yet neither provides a precise point. At small sizes, the unavailable-location icon also loses some visual specificity. No claim about human timing, user preference, or unknown-truth accuracy is made.
