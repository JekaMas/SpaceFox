# Blind image review

Reviewed all 60 images individually at their original supplied sizes (195, 222, or 390 pixels), without zooming, upscaling, or montages. Only order.json and the corresponding images were used as inputs. The answers were locked before viewing any truth, source metadata, or other review; none was viewed.

Observed response counts: 30 located, 27 none, 3 unable. These are response counts, not accuracy measurements.

- Bright white rings with gold centers and gold teardrop pins are the clearest visible geographic signs. A point on the ring or pin head was recorded in the actual image coordinate system.
- The explicit LAUNCH and СТАРТ labels make the intended launch meaning clear when a point is also visible.
- Unlabeled map points are easy to locate, but their launch function is inferred from the mission face rather than proved by a label. Their classification confidence is medium.
- Three globes show СТАРТ without a resolvable point. Those are unable, with null coordinates. The label alone was not used to invent a location.
- Small gold crossed-orbit or outlined icons have no visible geographic anchor and resemble status symbols. Faces containing only those symbols were recorded as having no visible launch-site marker.
- Dark map backgrounds and small globes can be visually decorative when no point is drawn. Native-size presentation limits certainty for the smallest examples.

No claims are made about human response time, human accuracy, preference, or agreement with unavailable truth.
