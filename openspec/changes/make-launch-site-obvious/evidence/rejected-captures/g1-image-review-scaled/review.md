# Independent image-only review

All 40 images were reviewed individually at their original 195 px size. Only the neutral order file and the corresponding image files were consulted. The answers were locked before any truth or source information was read.

Observed judgments: 35 located, 4 none, 1 unable. These are reviewer responses, not measured accuracy.

Pins and flags provide the most direct visible geographic cue. Bright rings and crosshair targets are also easy to locate as points on a map, although their launch-site meaning is inferred from their placement rather than an explicit label or legend. Diamond symbols are visible but communicate a generic selected location more strongly than a launch site specifically.

The smallest orange points and the blue concentric point are less visually prominent at 195 px. A separate rocket illustration makes a launch-site interpretation ambiguous when no distinct point marker accompanies it. On that image, the response is unable. The globe itself, country/place text, and a rocket illustration were not treated as sufficient evidence of a precise launch-site point.

Coordinates describe the visible marker centers in actual image pixels. No comparisons with unknown truth and no time or latency measurements were made.
