# Blind image review

Answers locked in `answers.json` after reviewing all 24 supplied images and before reading any truth or other reports. The review used only `order.json` and the corresponding images, displayed individually at their original 222 × 222 pixels. No zoom, upscale, or montage was used.

Coordinates are approximate marker centers in original image pixels, measured from the upper-left corner, with x increasing rightward and y increasing downward.

- 20 images contain a visible geographic point marker: 16 small circles/rings and 4 teardrop pins.
- 4 images contain no visible separate point marker. Their country/site text was not used as a substitute for a marker.
- Circular markers are clear as points but their launch-site meaning is implicit in the mission display. They have no attached explicit site label, so these classifications have medium confidence.
- Teardrop pins have a clearer geographic-location sign and received high confidence.

Qualitative issues: the circular markers are small at this size, and the map/globe often lies close to the right edge or under other dashboard elements. The bright center and pale outline distinguish the observed circles from map grid lines. A circle alone does not independently establish whether a point denotes an origin, destination, or another mission location; the launch-site interpretation depends on the visible launch-dashboard context. No source, truth, metadata, or other review report was consulted.
